# MUFG Outlook Copilot Demo – From Inbox to Impact

> Everything in this demo is fictional – FICTIONAL – FOR TRAINING USE ONLY. No MUFG or client data is used.

**Scenario:** You're **Thomas Brennan**, Relationship Manager at MUFG EMEA. It's **Friday 9 October 2026** and you're back after two days at a client event. Your Project Meridian folder is full: Fabrikam has asked MUFG to lead an €800m loan, five colleagues have sent updates, Tokyo Head Office wants a status report, and your boss, Haruka, wants a draft reply today.

**Goal:** catch up fast, check what Copilot used, reply well, and follow up without dropping anything.

**Timing (60 minutes):** opening 4 · story so far 3 · Part 1 13 · Part 2 14 · Part 3 11 · five-minute challenge 7 · wrap-up 8

## Story so far (30 seconds)

Fabrikam Industries, a long-standing client in Tokyo, wants to buy Contoso Energy Materials, a European battery-recycling company, and asks MUFG EMEA to lead an €800m loan. The Corporate Banking team has six weeks to check the deal and propose how much of the loan the bank keeps: €200m, €250m or €300m. Today's chapter: week 1, the request arrives.

## Demo setup

**Use:** [Meridian_Email_Pack.docx](../files/Meridian_Email_Pack.docx) – load emails E1 to E17 (leave out E18) into the demo mailbox **at least one day before**, then move them into a folder called **Project Meridian**.

## Part 1 – Catch up and check

**1. Summarise a long conversation**

**Where:** Email "Project Meridian – workstream updates" → **Summarise** at the top.

**Look for:** Credit Risk prefers keeping €200m because Industrials is close to its limit; Compliance lists open client checks; Operations gives account-opening lead times; the analyst sees leverage of about 2.7x; Thomas argues for €300m.

**Say:** One click gives you the whole conversation, including the views that disagree.

**2. Summarise across your emails**

**Where:** Copilot Chat in Outlook

```text
Summarise the emails in my Project Meridian folder about Fabrikam's financing request.
Group the summary by workstream – credit risk, compliance, operations, financial analysis and Tokyo Head Office –
and for each one list the decisions made, the open questions and who owns the next step.
```

**Look for:** five workstreams, each with decisions, open questions and an owner.

**Say:** A good prompt names the source, the structure and the output you need.

**3. Check which emails Copilot used**

**Where:** Copilot Chat in Outlook (same conversation)

```text
Which emails did you use for that summary? List each one with its sender, date and subject.
```

**Look for:** compare the list with the number of emails in the Project Meridian folder (17).

**Say:** For a broad question, Copilot works from the most relevant emails it finds, not necessarily every email. Narrow the question and check the list.

**4. Narrow the question**

**Where:** Copilot Chat in Outlook

```text
Summarise only the emails from Karim Benali in my Project Meridian folder and list the client documents he is waiting for.
```

**Look for:** Karim's open client checks and the documents he needs, due Friday 9 October.

**Say:** One sender, one thread or one folder at a time gives you a complete answer you can check.

**5. Find what needs a reply**

**Where:** Copilot Chat in Outlook

```text
Which emails in my Project Meridian folder need a reply from me, and by when? Put the most urgent first.
```

**Look for:** Haruka's request for a draft reply to Tokyo comes first. Tokyo needs it by 09:00 Tokyo time on Tuesday 13 October – that's 01:00 in London, so it must go on Monday. Next come Markus Brandt's meeting proposal and Karim's request for client documents.

**Say:** Open the thread before you act: something may already be answered further down.

**6. Check the facts**

**Where:** Copilot Chat in Outlook

```text
Compare the financing amounts mentioned in the Fabrikam emails and in the internal Project Meridian emails.
Are there any differences? Show which email each figure comes from.
```

**Look for:** "c. €750m" in Thomas's heads-up after a phone call (E1, Friday 2 October) against **€800m** in the client's written request (E2, Monday 5 October).

**Say:** The written request wins, and you confirm the amount with the client. Copilot finds the difference; you decide.

## Part 2 – Reply

**7. Reply to the client**

**Where:** Email E4 from Markus Brandt → **Reply all** (so Hayashi-san stays in copy) → **Draft with Copilot**

```text
Thank Mr Brandt and Hayashi-san for their request and confirm we are delighted to support.
Confirm the meeting on Tuesday 13 October at 10:00 London time and propose an agenda:
Fabrikam's strategic rationale, financing structure and timeline, hedging and cash management,
sustainability-linked features, and next steps.
Ask for Contoso's 2025 audited accounts and the group structure chart.
Use a formal, warm tone. Do not commit to any amount, hold size or terms.
```

Then change the tone to **Formal** and the length to **Short**, and select **Coaching by Copilot** for feedback on the draft.

**Look for:** no amount, hold size or terms in the draft.

**Say:** It's an external email: check the recipients, remove anything internal and make no commitments.

**8. Draft the update for Tokyo**

**Where:** Email E14 – FW: Fabrikam – request for status update → **Reply** → **Draft with Copilot**

```text
Draft a concise status update for Yamamoto-san, General Manager at Tokyo Head Office, for Haruka to review.
Use these headings: Opportunity, Proposed MUFG role, Amount and structure, Key risks under review, Timeline, Next update.
Formal and respectful tone, no more than 150 words.
State that MUFG's final hold is under review and will be confirmed after the Executive Committee on 12 November.
```

Refine it:

```text
Make it more concise and use bullet points under each heading.
```

**Look for:** no hold size, as Haruka asked.

**Say:** The draft follows the manager's instruction; you still read every line before it goes.

**9. Reply to the project group**

**Where:** Email E12 – the latest message in "Project Meridian – workstream updates" → **Reply all** → **Draft with Copilot**

```text
Thank everyone for their updates. Confirm that inputs for the Executive Briefing are due on Friday 23 October,
list each requested input with its owner in a short table, and invite questions before the client meeting on 13 October.
Collaborative, concise tone.
```

**Look for:** credit view – Elena; client checks and documents – Karim; onboarding lead times – Grace; financial model (16 October) – Sander; portfolio impact and hold options (22 October) – Priya.

**Say:** Owners and dates come from the emails; check them before you send.

## Part 3 – Prepare and follow up

**10. Prepare for the client meeting**

**Where:** Copilot Chat in Outlook

```text
I'm meeting Fabrikam on Tuesday 13 October. Based on the emails in my Project Meridian folder, prepare a one-page brief:
Fabrikam's objectives, what we know about the transaction, questions to ask the client,
internal positions I should be aware of, and topics I should not discuss externally.
```

**Look for:** under "not to discuss": internal ratings, sector limits, the €200m versus €300m debate, and any hold size.

**Say:** Copilot separates what you can say from what stays inside the bank. You make the final call.

**11. Follow up after the meeting** (it's now Tuesday 13 October)

**Where:** Email E17 – Notes – Fabrikam meeting this morning (rough) → **Forward** to Mr Brandt and Hayashi-san (don't send) → **Draft with Copilot**

```text
Using Olivia's notes below, draft a follow-up email to Mr Brandt and Hayashi-san that thanks them for today's meeting,
summarises what we discussed, lists the agreed next steps with owners and dates, and lists the open questions.
Where the notes do not show an owner or a date, mark it [to confirm].
Formal tone. Do not include any internal comments.
```

**Look for:** "[to confirm]" next to the hedging call and the early-December signing question.

**Say:** Delete Olivia's original notes below your draft: they contain internal remarks such as "still think we can push for €300m".

**12. Draft an agenda** (optional – you're now Olivia, Haruka's assistant)

**Where:** Copilot Chat in Outlook

```text
Draft a 90-minute agenda for the Project Meridian credit and compliance review on Tuesday 27 October,
based on the emails in my Project Meridian folder. Include the time for each item and its owner.
```

**Look for:** credit, compliance, operations and portfolio items, each with an owner.

**Say:** A first draft in seconds; the owners still agree their slots.

## Five-minute challenge

Haruka stops by: "What's still open on Project Meridian?"

```text
List every open action and unanswered question in my Project Meridian folder.
Show a table with the item, owner, due date and source email. Flag anything with no owner or no due date.
```

**Look for:** the Global Markets attendee "TBC" (E16); the hedging call and the early-December signing question with no owner (E17).

**Say:** Gaps found today don't become problems next week.

## Key takeaway

Copilot in Outlook turns a full inbox into a clear picture – what happened, what's open, who owns it – and gives you good first drafts for every audience. Narrow your questions, check which emails were used, and check the facts and recipients before anything is sent.
